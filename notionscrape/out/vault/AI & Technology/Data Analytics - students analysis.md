# Data Analytics - students analysis
> [!note] 
> Data collected from 01/2020 to 10/2024. Data Analytics applications that became students in European regions. More information on [metabase dashboard](https://metabase.lewagon.com/dashboard/297-applications-insights?application_date=2020-01-01~2024-10-14&training_type=Bootcamp&course=Data%20Analytics%20Bootcamp&region=France&region=Germany&region=Global&region=Southern%20Europe&region=UK&region=NECH&lead_source=&personal_source=&became_student%253F=true)
### 1. **Academic Level vs. Experience**:
- **Master’s degree holders** (41.8%) and **Bachelor’s degree holders** (28.7%) dominate the applicant pool.
        - These higher academic levels correlate strongly with personas like **Builder - Upskiller** (36.7%) and **Switcher - Meaning** (33.7%), which suggests that people with formal education are looking to either advance their current skills or switch careers for more fulfilling work.
        - **PhD holders** make up a smaller proportion (1.4%), potentially aligning with **Advanced-level personas**, such as **Builder - Upskiller** (3.6%) and **Switcher - Meaning** (1.6%).
        - Interestingly, **people with no qualifications or high school education** (7.5%) likely represent the personas looking to make major career shifts or start building skills from scratch, seen in categories like **Switcher - Low Earnings** or **Beginner-level personas**.
### 2. **Age vs. Experience and Persona**:
- The two dominant age groups, **22.5 years (30.4%) and 30 years (30.6%)**, represent young professionals in early to mid-career stages.
        - **Those aged 22.5 years** likely fall into the **Beginner-level personas**, such as **Switcher - Meaning** or **Builder - Upskiller**. Many might still be exploring their career paths or looking to switch.
        - **30-year-old applicants**, who are often more established in their careers, are likely to be interested in upskilling or switching for more meaningful work.
- **Older applicants (45+ years)** are relatively few (5.6%), which might indicate a smaller target group for career switchers at a later stage, often seen in **Advanced-level personas**.
### 3. **Gender vs. Persona**:
- **Men (55.3%)** form the majority of the applicants, but **women (38.5%)** also make up a significant portion. This suggests that while men are slightly more drawn to data analytics, there's a strong interest from women, particularly in the **Switcher - Meaning** or **Builder - Upskiller** personas.
- **Unknown gender** makes up **15.3%**, which suggests opportunities to personalise the user journey by addressing gender-specific concerns or content.
### 4. **Experience Level vs. Working Status**:
- **Unemployment** is high among applicants (**53.2%**). This group is likely looking for career shifts, seen in the dominance of **Switcher personas**. These applicants are likely to be **beginner-level learners** who are either making a transition into tech for the first time or **upskilling** to increase their job prospects.
- **Employed applicants (21%)** are likely aligned with the **Builder - Upskiller persona**, seeking to gain more competitive skills for career advancement.
- The smaller group of **self-employed applicants (4.6%)** may represent the **Builder - Entrepreneur persona**. These are individuals looking to apply data analytics to their own ventures or business operations.
### 5. **Academic Level vs. Application Sources**:
- **Word of mouth (52.1%)** is the largest application source across the board, but it’s likely to be even more relevant for **applicants with higher education**, like those holding a **Master’s or Bachelor’s degree**. People with formal education often trust recommendations from peers, alumni, or colleagues.
- **Online research (21.5%)** is another key source of applications. This method is more relevant to those who might be **younger (22.5 years)** or in the **student/graduate** category. These individuals are digitally savvy and more likely to seek educational options via online platforms or forums.
### 6. **Experience vs. Application Sources**:
- **Beginner-level personas** and those with **no experience** are more likely to hear about the bootcamp via **word of mouth** or **researching online**. They are likely more influenced by peer reviews, alumni success stories, and social proof.
- **Experienced or advanced applicants** are more likely to trust direct career advancement events, networking opportunities, or **job boards (2.6%)** as a source for finding programs like the bootcamp.
### 7. **Persona vs. Pre-Bootcamp Working Status**:
- **Unemployed applicants (53.2%)** predominantly align with **Switcher - Meaning** personas, looking for a fresh start or career transformation through education.
- **Employed applicants (21%)**, particularly those working full-time, are likely in the **Builder - Upskiller** category, seeking to gain further qualifications without a complete career switch.
- **Students/graduates (9.3%)** are likely part of **Beginner-level personas** or **Experiencers**, possibly exploring their next steps after education.
### Insights for Marketing and Targeting:
- **Personas to Target**:
        - Focus on the **Builder - Upskiller** and **Switcher - Meaning** personas, as they make up the majority of the applicant pool. Highlight **career advancement** and **meaningful career switching** in your messaging.
        - For **students or recent graduates**, consider targeted messaging around **exploring tech** and building relevant skills for the future.
- **Demographics**:
        - The largest age groups are around **22.5 to 30 years old**, a range that typically includes young professionals and early-career job seekers. Marketing should focus on **upskilling** and **career change opportunities** for this age group.
        - A balanced focus on both **genders** is important, but extra attention can be given to **women** and their interest in **career switching** or finding **meaningful work** through data analytics.
- **Application Source Strategy**:
        - **Word of mouth** is the strongest application driver, meaning alumni testimonials, referrals, and networking events should remain a critical part of marketing strategy.
        - **Online visibility** is crucial, as many applicants are researching independently. Strong SEO, detailed program pages, and content marketing around the bootcamp's impact can help capture this audience.
