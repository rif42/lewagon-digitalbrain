# Data Eng students analysis
> [!note] 
> From the launch in Q3, 2023. Data Engineering bootcamp students data from Europe and Global. [Metabase link ](https://metabase.lewagon.com/dashboard/297-applications-insights?application_date=2020-01-01~2024-10-14&training_type=Bootcamp&course=Data%20Engineering%20Bootcamp&region=France&region=Germany&region=Global&region=Southern%20Europe&region=UK&region=NECH&lead_source=&personal_source=&became_student%253F=true)
### Key takeaways
1. **Highly educated and experienced professionals** are the primary target, with a strong interest from those with master’s degrees or above, looking to upskill.
1. **The 30-year-old, employed professional** is the archetypal applicant. They prefer **online courses** for flexibility while continuing to work.
1. **Builder-upskillers** dominate the persona landscape, with a secondary focus on **entrepreneurs**. Both groups value the potential for growth and skill enhancement.
1. **Word of mouth** is a powerful driver of applications, but **online presence** is equally important for reaching younger or entrepreneurial students.
1. **Marketing campaigns should focus on flexibility, upskilling, and career enhancement**, with increased promotion during peak months (July, August, October).
### **1. Academic level & experience**
**Most applicants (67%) hold a master’s degree and have advanced experience (43%).**
- **Master’s degree applicants** dominate, particularly those with advanced experience (likely those looking to upskill or enhance their careers).
- **Intermediate level** applicants (28%) mainly hold either a master’s or bachelor’s degree and are primarily upskillers.
- **PhD holders** (5%) and **high school graduates** (2%) represent a small, niche group. These PhD candidates are likely pursuing high-level career shifts or specialised skills.
- **Beginners** and **those with no experience** typically hold lower academic qualifications (high school or unknown). These students may be using the program for career shifts rather than upskilling.
### Implications:
- The **core audience** is highly educated, seeking to upskill with advanced knowledge.
- The **program should be marketed towards professionals** with substantial education and experience who want to refine their skills.
---
### **2. Age distribution & experience**
**The typical applicant is 30 years old (57%) and has advanced or intermediate experience.**
- **30-year-olds** are mostly **advanced builder-upskillers**, reinforcing the idea that they are established professionals looking for an edge in their careers.
- **Younger applicants (22.5 years)**, forming 24%, are generally **intermediate upskillers** or **entrepreneurs**, signalling that some are in the early stages of their careers but want to build entrepreneurial ventures.
- **Older applicants** (45-52.5 years) are a small but notable group who may be using the program to switch fields or pivot to more technical roles.
### Implications:
- The program should be **targeted at professionals in their early 30s** with a clear focus on upskilling.
- There is potential to expand marketing towards **younger, entrepreneurial applicants** who are early in their careers but eager to grow.
---
### **3. Pre-bootcamp employment status & application source**
**Employed individuals dominate (66%), particularly for the online format (52%). Word of mouth is the largest application source (38%).**
- **Online programs** are a popular choice for employed applicants, likely due to the flexibility they offer.
- **On-campus programs** are more attractive to **unemployed and self-employed applicants**. This may reflect their desire for in-person networking and a more structured learning environment.
- **Word of mouth** drives most applications (38%), indicating that current students and alumni play a major role in attracting new students.
- **Online research** (13%) and **events** (7%) are important channels for reaching younger or entrepreneurial applicants.
### Implications:
- **Flexibility** should remain a key marketing message for employed professionals, especially for the **online format**.
- Strengthening **alumni networks and referral programs** could enhance word-of-mouth marketing.
- Invest in **content marketing** and **SEO** to capture more applicants through **online research**.
---
### **4. Personas & course timing**
**The majority of applicants are builder-upskillers (57%), applying consistently throughout the year.**
- **Builder-upskillers** are by far the largest persona group, especially among those with advanced experience and education. They are career professionals looking to enhance existing skills, not start from scratch.
- **Builder-entrepreneurs** represent 8%, showing some interest in using the bootcamp for launching ventures.
- **Switcher personas** (3%) and **no experience personas** (3%) tend to apply during the middle of the year, showing a desire for mid-career transitions.
- Application patterns are steady throughout the year, with slightly more activity in **October, July, and August**, suggesting these months could be peak times for targeted campaigns.
### Implications:
- Focus marketing efforts on **builder-upskillers** and **entrepreneurs**, highlighting the program’s potential to sharpen skills or build new ventures.
- Consistent demand means **year-round promotion** is necessary, with heightened activity during the mid-year and early fall months.
---
### **5. Sources of applications by Personas**
**Word of mouth is especially influential among builder-upskillers (57%).**
- **Builder-upskillers** tend to rely on **word of mouth** and **online research** when applying, while **entrepreneurs** also look at **events** and **online reviews**.
- **Tech employees** prefer online sources like **job boards** or **social media**, likely due to their familiarity with these channels.
- **Switcher personas** (meaning and low earnings) are split across sources, with no strong preference, but they may be influenced by funding options or career-focused resources like **job boards**.
### Implications:
- **Referral programs** and **testimonials** from current students or alumni could further strengthen **word-of-mouth marketing**.
- **Online presence** (SEO, reviews, social media content) needs to be maintained to attract **tech employees** and **entrepreneurs**.
