# Calling Applicants of Cancelled Batches - How to Give Bad News
> [!note] 
> The guide below covers** best practices** on how to go about contacts **for applicants of cancelled batches:** how to **analyze** **each** **case; **make sure that we **stay** **professional; **and **keep** **the** **applicant** **with** **us** for a future course 💪​
## 0. When are these calls needed? 📲​
---
- Closer to the end of a sales quarter, if a **given batch is far away from target and not feasible**, this batch might be cancelled. All teams work together to make sure that this very rarely has to happen.
- If a batch is cancelled** all students** of these batches **should be contacted to be notified that the batch will no longer happen** and offer alternatives. When this happens, usually there are cancellation calls by admissions that are very tiring, hard to manage, and hard to get started.
## 1. Key considerations ⚙️​
---
Before discussing best practices, some considerations that** should apply to all cancellation calls**:
- **Cancellations should always be thought together with alternatives** - You might have:
        - The same course in the near future in your city;
        - The same course in the same timeline in a different city of Le Wagon (especially Online);
        - A course that would still be a fit for the applicant’s motivations in your city;
        - All of these should be outlined per each canceled batch so that you have a full vision of alternatives. Noting down will help for when speaking with applicants.
- **Cancellation plans should always be thought together by the full team** - With GMs/CMs/Marketing/Admissions. Make sure to bring the full team into the discussion, thinking about alternatives. Don’t step in as the first person to cancel, nor do it before it’s a decision that is aligned across the full team.
- **Contractually, we can communicate a cancellation until 5-15 days** before the start of the course, depending on the contract sent. Be careful of this timing. Ideally, we’re always able to make a decision far before this date.
- Very important, **until a batch is cancelled, we should believe that it will happen**. We’ve seen hard turns of events with multiple last minute applications. If you don’t believe on a batch from the start selling uncertainty to our leads will immediately create a blocker for them and you won’t be able to have them in the batch.
## 2. Golden Rules / Best Practices🎖️​
---
1. **Always start by calling, never an email **📞** :** If the lead doesn’t pick-up, send them an email asking for a good time to call them. Sending an email instead of calling, whilst it might seem easier as there’s no confrontation, leaves you with no idea on their reaction and will leave the applicant feeling like the process was much less human;
1. **Don’t shy away from the true reason why you’re cancelling **❌: We should be honest and transparent. Start the call by saying that unfortunately we weren’t able to meet the minimum number of students to start the course, which wasn’t something we were expecting, as we’ve had a stable operation in the city since X and were prepared to run the class;
1. **Recognize the frustration and empathize with the lead **🐻‍❄️** :**** **Saying that this is a situation that can of course be stressful and frustrating and that we want to try and offer the best alternative possible. But recognize and say out loud that it is frustrating for the other person;
1. **Focus on the alternatives **🚦: After getting the intro done, focus on presenting the different alternatives that the applicant has. Remember that you should also say that a full reimbursement is granted if the applicant wants to get it;
1. **Analyze each process individually **🫀: You can predict the reaction of the applicant depending on your interview and process notes. Was this someone that left their job to do the bootcamp? Stress other courses we have on the same timeframe; Was this someone that wanted to eventually do the bootcamp and was not keen on timing? Stress the next batch we’ll have for the same course;
1. **Check your discount budget **💳** **: If you’re still before the 3% threshold, consider whether you should offer some discount for a future course / alternative. Important: This decision should be taken similarly for all applicants of a batch, don’t offer it to some and not to others;
1. **Send a follow-up email after the call **📩** :** List all alternatives that you outlined and send them by writing to the applicant. This will give them a formal point of consideration for other options. Also, note down on the email that the course they signed up for won’t be happening so that there is written cancellation proof;
1. **Schedule a reminder to do a follow-up call **☎️: Don’t be pushy, give them some time, but also don’t forget to follow-up on a decision of possible alternatives / refund;
1. **If the applicant doesn’t move forward with an alternative batch **🚫:** **Use the proper no-go reason to tag them as cancelled. If they speak about a future batch of interest, tag as postponed;
## 3. Framing & Preparing for the Call 📲​
---
**These calls are hard to do**. They can be stressful and can seem like there will be a lot of confrontation and weight on your shoulders when you’re doing them. After checking all the considerations and reading through the best practices, a few other notes below:
**What you should do on the call **✅​
- [ ] **Give yourself a few moments to get acquainted with the applicant again: **Don’t try to cover the true intention of the call with other motives, but take some initial time in the beginning to ask how the lead is going / ask about the prep-work. This will give you a better feel of the conversation;
- [ ] **Put yourself on the side of the applicant**: mention how you know that this situation is very frustrating and that we really wish they didn’t have to reorganize around this. Be human and transparent;
- [ ] **Prepare together with your other teammates some additional support:** if an applicant is e.g. on a cancelled Paris batch and will now do it in the same time in Nantes, accommodation links for Nantes / support from the city team will help make sure that they feel we still care for them and are professional;

---
**What you shouldn’t do on the call **❌​
- [ ] **Get into a legal / contract based speech**: It’s common to mention that the applicant is lucky we called early because the contract only says we need to cancel 5 days to go for the course. This will escalate the conversation quickly, avoid doing this or getting into legal talk;
