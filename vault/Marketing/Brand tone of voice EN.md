# **Le Wagon Brand Voice & Tone-of-Voice Guidelines**

Le Wagon is the trusted guide for anyone ready to make technology work for their future. ## **3. Core Brand Voice Characteristics**
### **The Four Voice Pillars**
**1. Inspiring & Action-Oriented**
Every piece of content should move someone. Whether it's to think differently, feel something, or take a next step. Inspiration without a direction is just noise.
**2. Clear & Accessible**
We make the complex feel achievable. Jargon earns its place only when it serves the reader. If a simpler word works, use it.
**3. Credibly Confident**
We don't shout. We don't over-promise. We state what we know to be true, backed by alumni outcomes, expert-built curriculum, and a global community. Our confidence comes from evidence, not bravado.
**4. Genuinely Human**
Le Wagon sounds like a real person wrote it. Not a committee, not an algorithm. Warm, specific, occasionally playful, and always on the side of the reader.

## **6. Do's and Don'ts**
### **Do**
- ✅ Lead with the human outcome, not the product feature ("Land your first Data Analyst role" not "Complete 400 hours of training")
- ✅ Use "you" and "your" generously
- ✅ Name the specific skill, role, or result
- ✅ Reference alumni outcomes and real proof points
- ✅ Use short sentences when you want impact; longer ones to explain
- ✅ End with a clear, specific next step
- ✅ Vary sentence length for rhythm
### **Don't**
- ❌ Use hollow enthusiasm ("Amazing!" "Incredible!" "Game-changing!")
- ❌ Over-explain on landing pages; hook first, detail later
- ❌ Use slang
- ❌ Use excessive exclamation marks (one per communication at most)
- ❌ Sound like a school or university
- ❌ Use fear as the primary motivator (urgency yes; fear-mongering no)
- ❌ Use passive voice when active is available
- ❌ Start with "We" on landing pages; start with "You" instead

## **8. Channel-Specific Guidelines**
### **Social Media (Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn)**
**Purpose**: Awareness and community building
**General Rules:**
- Humor and emojis are permitted on social; keep both accessible and non-offensive
- Social is the one channel where personality can stretch furthest
- No slang, ever
**Instagram**
- Tone: Warm, visual storytelling, community-first
- Format: Short, punchy captions with one clear idea per post
- CTAs: Soft ("Save this," "Tag someone who needs to hear this," "Link in bio")
- Emoji: Yes, used sparingly and purposefully (not decoratively)
**Facebook**
- Tone: Community-forward, slightly more conversational than LinkedIn
- Format: Can be longer than Instagram; suited to stories and testimonials
- CTAs: Moderate ("Learn more," "Join the community," "Share with someone ready to level up")
**LinkedIn**
- Tone: Professionally warm; thought leadership and proof-point heavy
- Format: Opens with a hook; structured with white space; ends with insight or question
- CTAs: Direct ("Apply now," "Download the syllabus," "Book a call")
- Emoji: Minimal; functional rather than expressive
**Social Do's:**
- ✅ Use alumni voices and direct quotes
- ✅ Reference real outcomes with real numbers
- ✅ Ask questions that invite engagement
- ✅ Use "we" to signal community
**Social Don'ts:**
- ❌ Multiple exclamation marks
- ❌ Generic motivational quotes without Le Wagon POV
- ❌ Posting without a clear message or purpose
### **Website & Landing Pages**
**Purpose**: Convert (syllabus download, event sign-up, application, contact)
**Golden Rule**: Hook and intrigue on the page. Let the next step (syllabus, form, application) deliver the full detail. Do not put everything on the page.
**Hierarchy of Copy:**
1. **Headline**: Lead with the transformation, not the product. ("Become a Data Analyst in 2 months." not "Our Data Analytics bootcamp lasts 8 weeks.")
1. **Subheadline**: Speak to the doubt or desire. ("No experience needed. Just the drive to build something real.")
1. **Body**: 2-3 benefit-led sentences maximum before a CTA
1. **CTA**: Specific, active, outcome-oriented ("Download the syllabus," "Apply for the next cohort," "Talk to an advisor")
**Voice:**
- Start with "You," not "We"
- Active voice always
- No corporate filler ("We are committed to...", "Our mission is to...")
- Short paragraphs; scannable structure
**CTAs by Funnel Stage:**
Funnel StageAppropriate CTAAwareness"Explore the curriculum" / "See what you'll build"Consideration"Download the full syllabus" / "Attend an info session"Decision"Apply for the next cohort" / "Talk to an advisor"
### **Email Communications**
**Purpose**: Nurture, convert, inform, re-engage
**Shared Voice Rules Across All Email Types:**
- Subject lines: curiosity-driven or outcome-driven; never clickbait
- Sign-offs: Warm, human, signed by a person (not "The Le Wagon Team" as default)
- No cold, transactional language in any email type
**Marketing Emails (Newsletters, Nurture, Campaigns)**
- Tone: Inspirational, curiosity-led, story-forward
- Structure: Hook → Insight or story → Relevant offer → Clear CTA
- Personality: This is where Le Wagon's warmth shows most
**Sales & Admissions Emails**
- Tone: Warm but efficient; direct without being cold
- Structure: Acknowledge where they are → Offer clear next step → Remove friction
- Key fix: Replace weak openers ("Just checking in...") with value-led ones ("You're one step away from seeing exactly what the Data Analytics program covers...")
- CTA: One per email, specific and action-oriented
**Transactional Emails (Confirmations, Reminders)**
- Tone: Clear, reassuring, human
- Even automated emails should feel like they were written by someone who cares
**Re-engagement Sequences**
- Tone: Non-judgmental, warm, curious
- Acknowledge the gap without guilt-tripping; lead with what's new or possible
**Email Don'ts:**
- ❌ "Just following up" as an opener
- ❌ Multiple CTAs competing in one email
- ❌ Signing off as a faceless entity
- ❌ Subject lines with false urgency or clickbait
### **Blog & Long-Form SEO Content**
**Purpose**: Authority building, organic discovery, top-of-funnel education
**Voice:**
- Every article should have a Le Wagon POV, not just a summary of what others have said
- Reference alumni outcomes, instructor expertise, and real bootcamp experiences wherever relevant
- End every article with a clear, contextually relevant CTA
**Structure:**
- Open with a hook that addresses a real question or tension your audience has
- Use subheadings that themselves tell a story (not just labels)
- Include at least one concrete example, stat, or alumni reference per section
- Close with a "so what" takeaway and a CTA
**The "So What" Test:** Every section should pass this test. If a reader could finish a paragraph and think "interesting, but what do I do with this?", rewrite it.
**SEO Integration:**
- Keywords should feel natural, never forced
- Prioritise clarity and reader value over keyword density
- Avoid writing that sounds optimised; write for humans first
- Don’t repeat the same keywords to reinforce semantic field (keyword stuffing), integrate synonyms where it makes sense
### **Company Announcements & News**
**Purpose**: Credibility, trust, milestone celebration
**Tone**: Step slightly more formal here, but never corporate. Le Wagon announcements should still feel like they come from a real organisation with personality.
**Do:**
- ✅ Lead with the impact on students or alumni, not the company achievement
- ✅ Include a human voice or quote
- ✅ Celebrate milestones with warmth, not self-congratulation
**Don't:**
- ❌ "We are pleased to announce..." (replace with something direct and warm)
- ❌ Jargon-heavy press language
- ❌ Announcements that celebrate Le Wagon without connecting the benefit to the reader
## **9. Before & After Voice Examples**
### **Example 1: Course Landing Page Headline**
**Before (off-brand):**
*"Our Data Analytics bootcamp is an intensive 8-week full-time program designed to give you the skills needed to enter the data industry."*
**After (on-brand):**
*"Become a Data Analyst in 8 weeks. Build real projects, master the tools employers actually use, and graduate with a portfolio that proves you can do the work."*
### **Example 2: Sales Email Opener**
**Before (off-brand):**
*"Hi [Name], just following up on your application to see if you had any questions."*
**After (on-brand):**
*"Hi [Name], you're one step away from seeing exactly what the Data Analytics program covers. Here's the full syllabus, so you can decide if it's the right fit before we talk."*
### **Example 3: LinkedIn Post**
**Before (off-brand):**
*"Le Wagon is proud to announce that we have helped over 20,000 students worldwide! We are committed to providing the best tech education possible. Apply today!"*
**After (on-brand):**
*"20,000 career transformations later, the one thing our alumni say most often isn't about the curriculum. It's about the people they met along the way. The global community you join at Le Wagon doesn't end at Demo Day. It's just getting started. Ready to be part of it?"*
### **Example 4: Blog Opening Paragraph**
**Before (off-brand):**
*"In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape, having tech skills has become increasingly important for professionals across all industries."*
**After (on-brand):**
*"A year ago, Marine was a marketing manager who spent half her week waiting on developers to pull reports she needed. Today, she pulls them herself, in minutes, and just got promoted. She's not an outlier. She's what happens when a non-technical professional learns to speak tech fluently."*
### **Example 5: Email Subject Line**
**Before (off-brand):**
*"Information about your Le Wagon application"*
**After (on-brand):**
*"Your next step (it takes 2 minutes)"*
