# Remote Bootcamp Operations
> [!note] 
> We are almost three weeks into our first remote bootcamp in Paris. You'll find below a few things that we have experimented and that have given good results so far. Hope this can be of some help. Good luck to you all 🚀​
## 🤗 Students Pre-Onboarding
---
> [!note] 
> We decided to invite the students on Slack **2 weeks prior** to the bootcamp start date.
The main goals were to:
→ Reassure students with the remote format and answer questions
→ Show extra support to make up for the current situation (TAs for prepwork completion)
→ Test their technical setup for remote classes (internet connection, computer, Zoom)
→ Lock them in by giving them a glimpse of the active community they were about to join
### **Pre-onboarding on Slack**
Here are the emails we used to communicate it with our enrolled students:
[No access](https://app.notion.com/p/96a588d9187e4b5b8f6e0f2bfdcb3646?pvs=25)
After they joined Slack, we invited them to take part in **Welcome Zoom calls **(one every 2 days) to:
→ Meet and greet, answer their questions
→ Test their technical setup
→ Give them a Slack tour (how it works, what the rules are etc.)
→ Share them this guide to make sure they get prepared
[Prepare for your first day](https://app.notion.com/p/lewagon/Prepare-for-your-first-day-c969c1ea232640208bb5a17c236e1d26?pvs=25)
From Monday to Friday, we booked one TA to be available on Slack from 10am to 5pm to answer questions and help students in completing their prep-work.
### **Pre-setup for non-Mac users**
A few days before the beginning of the bootcamp, teachers set up 1:1 meetings with non-mac users to make sure their computers were ready for the setup day. We went through the prerequisites as downloading the last updates for windows can be long. You will find more technical details [on this link](https://github.com/lewagon/setup/blob/master/WINDOWS.md#prerequisites).

### 👯‍♂️Buddy system
To reproduce the buddy system in a remote setting, we added the concept of **virtual tables** on Kitt. Each day, two pairs of buddies get matched to spend the day connected to the same whereby room.
This system prevents students from:
→ feeling isolated if their buddy is too shy, selfish or not communicative enough.
→ feeling too much friction due to constantly switching between slack calling his/her buddy and TAs.
This system allows students to:
→ Stay connected to their table-buddies (3) all day long
→ Help their table-buddies out while completing coding challenges
Most importantly,** it allows teachers and TAs to:
**→ Easily pop from one table to another, quickly get to see and talk to every student and make sure they are all correctly moving forward.
→ Help several students at the same time
→ Split the work by assigning TAs to each tables for the day.
![](https://app.notion.com/image/https%3A%2F%2Fs3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com%2Fsecure.notion-static.com%2F1e69657e-8e58-44eb-ae78-eaecbc408144%2FScreenshot_2020-04-21_19.22.43.png?table=block&id=cb7f5e40-33c7-482f-99f0-2ae92af4b126&spaceId=2b6dea13-478f-4f00-aff1-afd11ed5a03f&width=1120&userId=342d872b-594c-817d-8145-000204a2c537&cache=v2&imgBuildSrc=requestProxiedImageUrl)
We also use a Whereby room for the teaching team as it helps to better communicate and team-up to manage the class.

### 📝Quizzes
The quizzes are now editable pdfs that we can send via Slack. Students can complete it on their laptop and send it back to the teaching staff with an upload form in Kitt.
This allowed us to read every quiz and assign a teacher to each student for the correction.
We then reviewed every student's quiz on a 1:1 session through Slack calls.
### 🔌 **Reboot**
For the reboot group, we found out it was more practical to remain connected to a** Zoom Call** than to go back and forth between Zoom and Whereby tables.
In this setting, the teacher and TAs could easily switch between the coding session and the tickets. When a student needed help while completing the exercise, they would simply unmute themselves and say "I need help" and then someone from the team would call the student on Slack for an individual ticket.
Moreover, we realised that by putting students into rooms for the Reboot, some of them ended up not writing but just copy-pasting lines of code coming from their buddies. By staying all together on Zoom and helping them out individually, students ended up producing more code on their own which helped teachers learn more about the students' way of thinking.

### 🤝**Ice-breaker**
For our ice-breaking event, we used Zoom' **break-out rooms** feature with the automatic repartition of students between the rooms which worked very well! We chose 4 different subjects to drive conversations and for each subject we shuffled them into break-out rooms for 10 min (background, career plan, hobbies, a fun fact about themselves ...).
As an example, when they came back to the main session after the *fun fact* part, we asked one student per room to share with everyone the most surprising fun fact he/she had heard.

### 🥃 Remote Code'n'drink
The classic [[Code'n'Drink|💻Code'n'Drink]]** **event is a nice and animated event to launch your typical Friday's afterwork with. It is basically a code competition composed of several rounds where participants have to complete ruby challenges as fast as possible. To make things a little more festive, you can ask students to take a shot between each round to test their coding skills under extreme conditions 😉
To replicate this event in a remote setting, you can now use the new Code Tournament **feature** developed by the amazing tech team during the lockdown 😻It enables you to host the competition on Kitt, ask students to register, start/end each round, select the custom number of selected players, and showcase the players' screens live to their supportive buddies.
Start by inviting all the students to connect to Zoom, explain the rules of the game with a keynote, and launch the tournament from there.

### 🥗 Lunch breaks
We plan a Zoom Call everyday at lunch time to enable students to bond while eating their meal. (It appears that they rather tend to disconnect for lunch and join the call afterwards for coffee time between 1:30pm-2pm before getting back to their coding challenges).
It's a really good way for **the non-teaching team to connect with the students** everyday, get to know them, sound out their mood, get feedbacks etc.
What worked well was to organise a **themed lunch **around their** career project**. We used Zoom's breakout rooms to divide them into sub-groups: *entrepreneurs, developers, product managers, growth marketers, freelancers.*

### 🎤 Le Wagon **Talks**
We use Livestorm for our talks as it is a great tool for webinars and marketing purposes. However, it is not optimal to bring people together. Even if all your students are watching it at home, they may feel like they're watching it by themselves. Therefore, to enable students to **experience the talk all together** we prefer to broadcast it within a Zoom call by sharing our screen (and computer sound) to the students. It gives the student the feeling of being together and allows them to exchange in the Zoom chat while watching the talk.
We have done the same setting with our **live Alumni Q&A sessions** that we have set up on Instagram.
